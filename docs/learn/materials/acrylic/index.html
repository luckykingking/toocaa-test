Acrylic Laser Cutting & Engraving Guide

Choose acrylic for your laser, find L2 black acrylic settings, and prepare a small test for jewelry, signs, and decorative pieces.

Working with acrylic

Acrylic response depends on color, transparency, thickness, and laser wavelength. A dark acrylic example in a diode laser table does not establish that clear, blue, white, or mirrored acrylic will behave the same way. Confirm the exact sheet is suitable for your module before buying a large batch.

Cast, extruded and clear acrylic

TOOCAA's acrylic guide notes that cast stock generally gives stronger engraving contrast than extruded stock. Color and transparency are separate choices: clear acrylic is not a suitable cutting target for the visible-diode workflow described here. Do not copy a CO2 clear-acrylic project to a diode machine or keep increasing exposure to make a transparent sheet absorb the beam.

Check the material before processing

Ask for the material identity and laser suitability rather than relying on the name 'plastic.' Check masking and backing as well as the sheet itself. Never substitute PVC, vinyl, or an unidentified plastic for acrylic.

Prepare a useful test

A material test is useful only when it represents the final job. Use the same sheet or batch, record the laser module and software units, and change one setting at a time. Keep the sample after the job so you can compare a later delivery against it.

  1. Confirm the sheet is acrylic and suitable for the laser wavelength.
  2. Test an offcut of the actual color and thickness.
  3. Check both the front and underside after the sample cools.
  4. Measure holes and slots before nesting jewelry or sign components.

Inspect the result

Melting, a cloudy edge, or a shallow mark can have different causes. Verify compatibility and focus first; then test a small piece using a row that matches the machine and material. Record each change and avoid repeated passes on a visibly overheating sample.

Choose settings for the exact machine

Use the manufacturer's material table for the exact laser module and processing mode. Speed in mm/min is not the same as mm/s, and an engraving row is not a cutting instruction. If the material, thickness, or surface does not match a published entry, treat it as an undocumented combination and seek model-specific guidance before continuing.

These settings are for the TOOCAA L2 configurations listed below. Match the material, thickness, module, and operation, then test an offcut before starting your project. For NOVA, use the settings in the NOVA documentation. All speeds below are in mm/min.

Inspect edge quality after a small cut

Let the test cool, then examine the edge, corners, holes and underside. A smooth-looking edge does not prove the hole or slot is the right size. Rounded melted corners, sticking pieces or smoke staining call for checking compatibility, duplicate paths, focus, airflow and the matching source settings. A polished appearance is an outcome to test, not a guaranteed finish for every acrylic color.

Frequently asked questions

Can every acrylic product use the same laser settings?

No. Composition, thickness, surface treatments, and the laser module change the result. Test the actual stock and use a matching manufacturer reference.

Should I increase power if the first test fails?

Check compatibility, focus, workholding, and speed units first. Stop if the material overheats, produces unexpected fumes, or is not documented for the process.

Can I cut clear acrylic with these diode settings?

No. The linked TOOCAA acrylic guide distinguishes clear acrylic from suitable opaque stock and directs clear-acrylic work to an appropriate CO2 process.
