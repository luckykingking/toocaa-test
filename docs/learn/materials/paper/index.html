Paper Laser Cutting & Engraving Guide

Set up laser-cut cards and paper details with careful material selection, secure workholding, and small test designs.

Working with paper

Plain paper and cardstock are useful for folded cards, invitations, stencils, and packaging prototypes. Foils, glitter, laminates, coatings, and adhesive layers can change both the result and suitability. Verify the complete construction rather than treating every decorative paper as plain cardstock.

Check the material before processing

Keep sheets flat and prevent movement from airflow. Choose a design with enough bridges to hold delicate details together. Remove scraps from the bed and confirm extraction before testing; paper is combustible and needs continuous attendance during processing.

Prepare a useful test

A material test is useful only when it represents the final job. Use the same sheet or batch, record the laser module and software units, and change one setting at a time. Keep the sample after the job so you can compare a later delivery against it.

  1. Check coatings and adhesive with the supplier.
  2. Test a short straight cut and a small curve.
  3. Review the underside and fragile details after cutting.
  4. Make a full-scale fold test before committing to printed stock.

Inspect the result

Overlapping vectors can cut the same line more than once. Check the artwork for duplicate paths and tiny loose islands before changing settings. Brown edges, lifting corners, and disappearing details need attention to dwell, support, and design size.

Choose settings for the exact machine

Use the manufacturer's material table for the exact laser module and processing mode. Speed in mm/min is not the same as mm/s, and an engraving row is not a cutting instruction. If the material, thickness, or surface does not match a published entry, treat it as an undocumented combination and seek model-specific guidance before continuing.

No matching numerical row is included for this material in the retained L2 data. Do not calculate a substitute for another machine or stock. Check the current TOOCAA material reference.

Frequently asked questions

Can every paper product use the same laser settings?

No. Composition, thickness, surface treatments, and the laser module change the result. Test the actual stock and use a matching manufacturer reference.

Should I increase power if the first test fails?

Check compatibility, focus, workholding, and speed units first. Stop if the material overheats, produces unexpected fumes, or is not documented for the process.
