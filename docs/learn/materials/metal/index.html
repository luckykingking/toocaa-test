Metal Laser Marking: Surfaces & Preparation

Understand metal marking limits, distinguish surface marking from cutting, and prepare a model-specific test.

Working with metal

Surface marking, removing a coating, and cutting metal are different jobs. A desktop diode laser marking example is not evidence that it can cut sheet metal. Match the metal, coating, laser module, and intended process to the manufacturer's documentation.

Match the metal surface to the laser module

A marking result depends on the source and the surface. TOOCAA publishes separate metal guidance for its machines and discusses its infrared module for metal-focused work. Do not infer a bare-metal cutting capability from a surface-marking example.

Surface or systemCheck firstDo not assume
Anodized aluminumCoating identity, contrast and finish after cleaningThat the same result applies to bare aluminum
Coated or painted metalAll coating ingredients and a documented processThat any paint or marking spray is laser-safe
Stainless steelExact module, finish and manufacturer referenceThat an unknown grade or finish uses one universal setting
Infrared / diode / fiber systemsThe actual laser source and supported operationThat equal advertised watts mean identical settings or capability

Check the material before processing

Identify whether the workpiece is bare metal, painted, coated, or anodized. Check any marking product separately and follow its supplier's instructions. Reflective surfaces and unknown coatings need a material-specific assessment before exposure to the beam.

Prepare a useful test

A material test is useful only when it represents the final job. Use the same sheet or batch, record the laser module and software units, and change one setting at a time. Keep the sample after the job so you can compare a later delivery against it.

  1. Verify that the documented operation is marking or engraving, not cutting.
  2. Use a matching surface and coating for the sample.
  3. Compare small text and filled areas after cleaning.
  4. Keep the protective enclosure and other safeguards in place.

Inspect the result

A mark that looks dark under one light may have weak contrast or poor wear resistance under another. Inspect the result after cleaning and under normal use conditions. Do not increase exposure indefinitely when a material is outside the machine's supported process.

Choose settings for the exact machine

Use the manufacturer's material table for the exact laser module and processing mode. Speed in mm/min is not the same as mm/s, and an engraving row is not a cutting instruction. If the material, thickness, or surface does not match a published entry, treat it as an undocumented combination and seek model-specific guidance before continuing.

No matching numerical row is included for this material in the retained L2 data. Do not calculate a substitute for another machine or stock. Check the current TOOCAA material reference.

Make a useful metal-marking sample

Use the same surface finish as the final blank. Include small text, a fine line and a filled shape. Compare the mark under normal viewing angles after the approved cleaning step. If a marking product is involved, follow its own application and safety instructions. A dark fresh mark is not by itself proof of wear resistance or food-contact suitability.

Frequently asked questions

Can every metal product use the same laser settings?

No. Composition, thickness, surface treatments, and the laser module change the result. Test the actual stock and use a matching manufacturer reference.

Should I increase power if the first test fails?

Check compatibility, focus, workholding, and speed units first. Stop if the material overheats, produces unexpected fumes, or is not documented for the process.
