Leather Laser Cutting & Engraving Guide

Prepare leather tags and engraved accessories with material checks, test pieces, and a repeatable finishing workflow.

Working with leather

Leather can be used for tags, labels, patches, and accessories, but tanning methods, dyes, finishes, and synthetic layers matter. A natural appearance does not prove that a sheet is suitable for laser processing. Identify its composition and obtain the supplier's laser guidance first.

Check the material before processing

Check the hide or sheet for coatings, synthetic backing, adhesive, and treatment. Do not laser-process PVC-based imitation leather or unknown composites. Choose a small offcut from the same batch and arrange extraction before the test.

Prepare a useful test

A material test is useful only when it represents the final job. Use the same sheet or batch, record the laser module and software units, and change one setting at a time. Keep the sample after the job so you can compare a later delivery against it.

  • Confirm composition and laser suitability with the supplier.
  • Hold the offcut flat without putting fixtures in the beam path.
  • Test text, a hole, and a short outline separately.
  • Check cleaning and finish compatibility before making the full batch.

Inspect the result

Fine text can lose definition when the surface scorches, while cut edges can deposit residue. Start with simple lettering, inspect the mark after cleaning, and check that the finished piece does not transfer residue onto fabric or skin.

Choose settings for the exact machine

Use the manufacturer's material table for the exact laser module and processing mode. Speed in mm/min is not the same as mm/s, and an engraving row is not a cutting instruction. If the material, thickness, or surface does not match a published entry, treat it as an undocumented combination and seek model-specific guidance before continuing.

Frequently asked questions

Can every leather product use the same laser settings?

No. Composition, thickness, surface treatments, and the laser module change the result. Test the actual stock and use a matching manufacturer reference.

Should I increase power if the first test fails?

Check compatibility, focus, workholding, and speed units first. Stop if the material overheats, produces unexpected fumes, or is not documented for the process.
