Stone & Slate Laser Engraving Guide

Prepare slate and stone engraving tests, choose legible artwork, and compare surface contrast before engraving a finished piece.

Working with stone

Slate and other stone surfaces can produce useful engraved marks, but mineral composition, texture, sealant, and natural variation affect contrast. Choose engraving blanks with a known surface and test the same material as the final piece.

Check the material before processing

Check the blank for unstable layers, cracks, and coatings. Support it so that the top surface stays level. A rough or curved surface can move out of focus across the image even when its center is positioned correctly.

Prepare a useful test

A material test is useful only when it represents the final job. Use the same sheet or batch, record the laser module and software units, and change one setting at a time. Keep the sample after the job so you can compare a later delivery against it.

  • Use an inexpensive blank from the same batch.
  • Level the engraving area and set focus to that surface.
  • Compare simple line art with a filled sample.
  • Keep a reference photo and the file used for each test.

Inspect the result

Small gray details in a photograph may disappear on textured stone. Begin with text or a simple high-contrast graphic and compare it with a small image sample. Inspect after cleaning before deciding whether any finish is appropriate for the item.

Choose settings for the exact machine

Use the manufacturer's material table for the exact laser module and processing mode. Speed in mm/min is not the same as mm/s, and an engraving row is not a cutting instruction. If the material, thickness, or surface does not match a published entry, treat it as an undocumented combination and seek model-specific guidance before continuing.

Frequently asked questions

Can every stone product use the same laser settings?

No. Composition, thickness, surface treatments, and the laser module change the result. Test the actual stock and use a matching manufacturer reference.

Should I increase power if the first test fails?

Check compatibility, focus, workholding, and speed units first. Stop if the material overheats, produces unexpected fumes, or is not documented for the process.
