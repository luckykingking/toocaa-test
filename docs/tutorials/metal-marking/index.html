Prepare a Metal Marking Test

Distinguish marking from cutting, confirm surface compatibility, and evaluate a small metal marking sample after cleaning.

Identify the surface operation

Removing a coating and marking bare metal are different processes. Check the exact metal, coating, laser module, and any approved marking product. A desktop diode marking example does not establish metal cutting capability.

Work through the job

Use the following sequence with the current instructions for the exact machine. Keep the first job small enough to inspect clearly and leave time to check the result before repeating it.

  1. Confirm a documented process for the metal and module.
  2. Identify coatings and follow any approved preparation instructions.
  3. Keep protective safeguards in place around reflective objects.
  4. Test small text and a filled patch on a matching sample.
  5. Inspect the cleaned result under ordinary lighting.

Decide what a successful mark means

A dark sample may not have the wear resistance or contrast a finished product needs. Compare readability after cleaning and normal handling. If the manufacturer does not document the material-process combination, seek guidance rather than escalating exposure.

Save the setup for the next job

Keep the artwork version, stock details, software version, laser module, focus method, speed units, power, passes, and a photo of the finished test together. If any of those change, run a fresh check rather than assuming the old result still applies.
