Laser Cutting Paper & Cardboard

Prepare flat stock, clean vectors, and a small fold or slot test for cards, stencils, and packaging prototypes.

Prepare the file and the sheet together

Paper details need enough bridges to hold together at the chosen size. Check duplicate vectors, tiny islands, and the order of operations before changing machine settings. Plain cardstock is not interchangeable with laminated, foil-backed, or adhesive stock.

Work through the job

Use the following sequence with the current instructions for the exact machine. Keep the first job small enough to inspect clearly and leave time to check the result before repeating it.

  • Verify the complete paper or board composition.
  • Clear scraps and secure the sheet against movement.
  • Check duplicate lines and small loose details in the file.
  • Test a curve, straight cut, and any fold or slot.
  • Stay present and inspect the top and underside after cutting.

Check the assembled result

A packaging outline is only useful when it folds and fits correctly. Measure the actual board thickness, assemble an inexpensive full-scale prototype, and check closure and removal of the item. Fire risk and airflow need attention even on a short paper job.

Save the setup for the next job

Keep the artwork version, stock details, software version, laser module, focus method, speed units, power, passes, and a photo of the finished test together. If any of those change, run a fresh check rather than assuming the old result still applies.
