Flame Detection Alarm: Checks Before Restarting

Treat a flame warning as a safety event, inspect only after the area is safe, and do not disable detection to finish a job.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Do not assume the warning is false

A brief flame, hot residue or a small fire may no longer be visible when you reach the machine. Stop processing using the documented controls and follow the machine's fire-safety procedure. Keep people clear and do not restart to prove that the alarm was unnecessary.

Identify the machine and warning

The L2 alarm reference lists one long beep followed by four short beeps for flame detected in the working area. That is an L2 reference, not a NOVA sound-code table. For NOVA, preserve the actual touchscreen message and the operating stage. Do not silence the warning before recording what triggered it.

Inspect only after the work area is safe and cool

Disconnect power before maintenance or reaching into the machine.

  1. Check for charred scraps, deposits or damage around the bed and workpiece.
  2. Verify the material identity, coating and backing instead of relying on its appearance.
  3. Review duplicate paths, repeated passes and the selected cutting/engraving mode.
  4. Check the accessible air and exhaust routes for blockage using their maintenance instructions.

Recurring alarms need a cause, not a bypass

If the alarm repeats without an understood and corrected cause, leave the machine out of processing service and contact support. Provide the original file, stock details, settings and warning record. Do not cover a detector, change unapproved sensitivity values or run unattended. An alarm disappearing after a restart does not establish that the material or setup is safe.

Frequently asked questions

Can I turn off flame detection if I see no flame?

No. A visual check does not prove the event was false. Resolve the condition or get model-specific support without defeating detection.
