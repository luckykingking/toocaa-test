Bands or Stripes in Laser-Engraved Photos

Distinguish scan-line gaps, image-processing artifacts and movement errors when a photo engraving shows visible bands.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Look at the band direction

Photograph the engraving under even light and compare it with the image preview at finished size. Regular gaps parallel to the scan lines suggest a different issue from doubled edges or a repeated mechanical shift. If the bands are already visible in the source image, changing the machine will not restore the missing tones.

Compare one small image region

Use a crop containing a smooth tone, a dark edge and a lighter area on the same material batch. Avoid reprocessing a whole portrait for every change.

  1. Keep the crop size, material, focus and power/speed combination recorded.
  2. Compare the original image and the exported image for posterization.
  3. Check that the image layer uses the intended image or dithering mode.
  4. Change only one image-processing or scan-spacing control for each sample.

Compare Jarvis, Stucki and grayscale deliberately

In LightBurn, Jarvis and Stucki turn image tones into dot patterns. Its image-mode reference describes Jarvis as slower to process but better at retaining midtone shading, while Stucki processes faster. Grayscale instead varies laser power between the layer's minimum and maximum settings. Compare one mode at a time on the same small tonal crop and stock. Do not combine a mode change with new dimensions, line spacing and exposure, or assume these LightBurn controls have identical names in Studio.

Check line interval in LightBurn

Line interval controls the distance between scan lines. Excessively close lines can overlap and lose detail; wide spacing can leave visible gaps. LightBurn provides an Interval Test for selecting spacing on the actual laser/material combination. Use that test and inspect the individual lines rather than assuming that the highest DPI gives the best photo.

Keep image settings separate from motion faults

If vector lines are also wavy or displaced, investigate positioning and motion before adjusting the photograph. Save the test crop and the layer settings so the result can be reproduced. Do not compensate for slipping material or erratic movement with extra sharpening or exposure.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher DPI always remove banding?

No. DPI and line interval affect scan density. Too much overlap can darken or blur the image; the useful interval must be tested on the real material.
