Uneven Laser Cutting Depth Across a Sheet

Find out whether uneven cuts follow the wood, the work surface, or a particular part of the machine before changing power.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Map where the cut changes

Keep the first sample and mark which edge faced the front of the machine. A cut that succeeds in one corner but not another is different from a cut that fails at a glue pocket. Photograph both faces and note whether the sheet was bowed, supported unevenly, or thicker at the failed area.

Check the sheet and support

Use a flat, identified offcut with a consistent thickness. A bow changes the distance from the head to the surface. Loose scraps underneath a panel can lift one corner even when the panel looks level from above.

  1. Stop the job and let the material cool before removing it.
  2. Measure the stock at several positions and remove loose debris from the bed.
  3. Support the sheet using the working-panel arrangement specified for the machine.
  4. Keep clamps and fixtures outside the planned head movement.

Set focus for the actual surface

On L2, set the focusing rod against the workpiece and secure the module as described in the L2 FAQ. On NOVA, focus on the actual stock, then check the refreshed camera overlay. Camera calibration and laser focus solve different problems; moving artwork cannot correct a changing focal distance.

Compare a small repeated pattern

When the machine and stock are safe, place the same simple test at separated positions on one flat offcut. Keep the file and settings unchanged. If the weak area moves with a rotated piece of wood, examine the stock. If it stays in the same bed position on multiple flat sheets, send the pattern and photographs to support. Do not attempt undocumented gantry or bed adjustments.

Frequently asked questions

Should I increase power only in the weak corner?

First rule out bowing, thickness changes and focus differences. A location-dependent fault on several flat sheets needs a machine inspection, not a hidden power correction in the artwork.
