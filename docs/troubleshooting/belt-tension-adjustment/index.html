Check a Loose Belt Before Adjusting Tension

Distinguish a visibly loose or damaged belt from a file or workholding error and obtain the correct adjustment procedure for the machine.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Confirm that the belt is involved

Misaligned artwork does not by itself prove incorrect belt tension. Check whether a blank moved, an origin changed or the head hit an obstruction. A damaged belt, visible slack or repeatable lost motion is more specific evidence. Keep the machine stopped for grinding or skipped movement.

Inspect only with power disconnected

Follow the model's access instructions and do not pull on cables or force an axis.

  1. Look for damaged teeth, fraying, a displaced belt or an obvious obstruction.
  2. Check whether a recent accessory or assembly change preceded the problem.
  3. On NOVA, use the slide-rod maintenance guidance for the permitted movement and belt inspection.
  4. Record the affected axis and photograph visible damage without dismantling the drive.

Use the model-specific adjustment

NOVA's rod-maintenance guide says to follow the corresponding adjustment instructions when a belt is loose. It does not specify a universal screw-turn count, force or belt frequency. Do not tighten until the belt feels like a string, move a pulley to compensate for a skewed frame, or apply an unrelated printer/CNC tension rule. Obtain the correct procedure for the exact hardware revision from TOOCAA when it is not available with the machine.

Check the result with a small geometry test

After a documented adjustment and full reassembly, confirm normal homing and frame movement. Compare a simple small outline with the retained baseline. Continued skipping, unequal geometry or unusual noise means the cause is not resolved. Do not add more tension repeatedly to conceal another mechanical fault.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one correct number of turns for every TOOCAA belt?

No. Hardware and adjustment points differ. Use the exact model procedure rather than an arbitrary screw-turn count or tension-by-feel rule.
