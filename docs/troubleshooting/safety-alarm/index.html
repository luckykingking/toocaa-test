Safety Alarm or Protective Cover Warning

Record the alarm and follow the documented stop and recovery procedure while keeping every protective system active.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Identify the symptom

A safety warning may indicate a cover, motion, heat, extraction, or other model-specific condition. Read the exact message rather than assuming that an alarm with a similar appearance has the same cause.

Use the right warning reference

L2 has published alarm descriptions. NOVA's touchscreen message and model documentation should guide its diagnosis. A factory reset, sensor bypass or repeated start attempt is not a substitute for resolving the condition that triggered an alarm.

Checks in order

Begin with the visible status, configuration, and material. Keep a note of each check and its result. Inspect hardware only with the machine stopped and power disconnected as directed by the manual.

  1. Stop the job and make the work area safe.
  2. Record the exact message and what happened immediately before it.
  3. Check normal cover closure and visible obstructions without bypassing sensors.
  4. Use the model-specific recovery procedure only after the cause is addressed.
  5. Contact support if the warning returns or a safeguard appears damaged.

Stop and seek support when needed

Do not defeat an interlock, bridge a sensor, or suppress a warning to complete a job. A recurring safety fault should remain out of service until resolved.

What the check tells you

Choose the next step from the observed result, keeping a record of what actually changed.

Observed resultNext step
A hazard or damaged safeguard remainsLeave the machine out of processing service.
Only an L2 beep reference is availableDo not use it as a decoder for NOVA.
The same warning returns after normal checksRequest support with the full message and operating stage rather than bypassing it.

What to include in a support request

Send the exact model and module, software version, material identity, file details, job settings with units, and the full error message. Describe the last successful job and any recent change. Include clear photos when safe; do not reproduce a hazardous event to record it.
