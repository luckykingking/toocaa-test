Engraving Too Light or Too Dark

Separate image contrast, exposure, and material variation when an engraving loses detail or appears too faint.

Applies to: TOOCAA L2 / TOOCAA NOVA

Identify the symptom

Inspect the file preview and the material separately. A dark photograph can lose midtones before it reaches the laser, while a material surface may produce weak contrast even with a sound file. Keep image edits and machine adjustments in separate tests.

Keep scan spacing and exposure separate

In image work, scan-line interval affects how adjacent lines overlap. LightBurn's Interval Test can compare useful spacing on the actual stock. Keep a small tonal crop and record its image mode, size, interval, speed and power rather than changing all controls together.

Checks in order

Begin with the visible status, configuration, and material. Keep a note of each check and its result. Inspect hardware only with the machine stopped and power disconnected as directed by the manual.

  1. Compare the original image with the processed preview.
  2. Check the operation mode, power, speed units, and focus.
  3. Use a small matching blank with a simple text or filled sample.
  4. Change one setting or image-processing factor at a time.
  5. Inspect after cleaning at the intended viewing distance.

Stop and seek support when needed

Higher resolution or repeated exposure does not guarantee more detail. Stop when the surface overheats or the process is outside documented material compatibility.

What the check tells you

Choose the next step from the observed result, keeping a record of what actually changed.

Observed resultNext step
The exported preview already lacks detailReturn to photo preparation instead of compensating with laser power.
Visible regular scan gaps remainTest scan spacing using the image/interval guidance.
One stock batch differs from anotherRetest the actual surface and keep the same file and dimensions.

What to include in a support request

Send the exact model and module, software version, material identity, file details, job settings with units, and the full error message. Describe the last successful job and any recent change. Include clear photos when safe; do not reproduce a hazardous event to record it.
